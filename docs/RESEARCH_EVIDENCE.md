## 1. Why seasonal winter roads matter
- Canada's winter road network is estimated at about 10,000 km (Transportation Association of Canada paper on winter roads and climate adaptation, tac-atc.ca; CHECK exact wording and year).
- Winter roads are a main way to move supplies to remote northern communities, and ClimateData.ca states they are the most economical means of transport for those communities (climatedata.ca, "Winter Ice Roads in Northern Ontario").
- Winter roads in Ontario's Far North are described as a critical seasonal lifeline for remote First Nation communities (Hori et al. 2018, Climatic Change 148:109-122, https://doi.org/10.1007/s10584-018-2178-2).
- For our case: the Dettah ice road is a short convenience route over Yellowknife Bay with an all-season alternative (Cabin Radio, 2022 to 2025). Be honest about this on the website; do not call it a lifeline.

## 2. How climate change may affect the conditions that support those roads
- ClimateData.ca states northern Canada is warming about three times as fast as the global average (https://climatedata.ca/resource/overview-of-available-western-science-based-climate-data-for-northern-canada/). CHECK underlying reference.
- Hori et al. found decreasing freezing degree-days and increasing monthly mean temperatures in the western James Bay region over 1961 to 2014 (Theoretical and Applied Climatology 129:1309-1320, https://doi.org/10.1007/s00704-016-1855-1).
- Rising temperatures and shifting freeze-thaw have been linked to delayed openings and shorter seasons for the Tibbitt to Contwoyto winter road (Song et al., Cold Regions Science and Technology, via the Oxford University Research Archive; CHECK full citation).

## 3. What prior research has established
- **Hori et al. (2015/2017), James Bay Winter Road:** FDD trends declined significantly, but no statistically significant link between opening dates and FDD was found, possibly because few opening dates were available; Oct to Dec FDD was more closely linked to opening dates than FDD accumulated up to the opening date. We cannot claim to be first to analyse freezing accumulation and winter-road opening dates.
- A minimum of about 380 FDD before the opening has been proposed for the James Bay Winter Road (ClimateData.ca summary of Hori et al.). This is specific to that road.
- Song et al. use FDD as a key indicator for the opening dates and cumulative downward thermal radiation for the closing dates of the Tibbitt to Contwoyto winter road (Oxford). Suggests radiation could matter for closing dates; we do not use radiation (scope).
- Dettah ice road context: the territory requires about 34 to 36 cm of ice before opening (NNSL); snow insulation and overflow have delayed openings (CBC, Cabin Radio 2023). Climate is one of several drivers.

## 4. What existing tools offer
- ClimateData.ca provides climate data, indices and projections for Canada. We did not find, in a short search, a public tool that combines NASA daily temperature trends, significance, the actual NWT opening and closing records, and uncertainty in one accessible place. This is a limited search, not an exhaustive review; say "to our knowledge".

## 5. What NorthLink specifically adds
An accessible, reproducible, location-specific climate trend detective that shows (a) how freezing conditions at two NWT ice-road locations are changing (NASA data), (b) whether each trend is statistically supported, with uncertainty, (c) how the two locations differ, and (d) how the recorded opening and closing dates have changed, with a clearly labelled exploratory link between them.
Already established from road records alone (our calculation, 32 winters): Dettah opening about 9 days later per decade and the open-to-close interval about 10 days shorter per decade; Aklavik opening about 7 days earlier per decade and the interval about 8 days longer (Aklavik's opening trend has a CI touching 0). Different directions at two roads are a finding that deserves caution, not a contradiction.

## 6. Why it may be useful for adaptation discussions
It gives communities and planners an open, reproducible way to look at local temperature and road-season history together. Say "may help inform discussion"; do not say the tool has been used or validated by communities or road operators (no evidence yet).

## 7. What NorthLink cannot determine
Ice thickness or safety; future opening dates; the cause of any road-date change (construction, snowfall, overflow, rules and decisions also matter); usable days; conditions at every point on the road; results for other roads.
